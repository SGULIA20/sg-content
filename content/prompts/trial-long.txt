

NEW PICTURE STYLE FOR THIS LONG VIDEO (these rules OVERRIDE the picture rules above wherever they differ; founder, 10 Nov 2026: the same new style as the Shorts):
- The video is shown FULL SCREEN, 16:9, with no title band: every scene's `visual.prompt` describes one REALISTIC, photographic-looking view (a wide landscape, an aerial view, a close view of stone, a document or an object, people seen as in a documentary photograph, a street, a shop, a bank counter) with natural light. Say "photorealistic" and describe the place, the people and the moment. No cartoon, comic or drawing words, no named cast characters, no text, numbers, screens, charts or logos in the picture. Deities as before: from behind, at a distance, or as a statue or painting in the scene.
- Keep the lower fifth of each picture calm (ground, water, floor, sky): the captions sit there.
- A scene set in the past is shown as it was THERE (24 Nov 2026: both channels now take subjects from the whole world). An Indian story is ancient India (Indian people, dhotis and sarees, bows and spears, bronze armour, Indian huts and temples), never European clothes, knights or castles. A Greek, Egyptian, Chinese, Norse, Mayan, African or any other people's story keeps that people's own dress, buildings and landscape. A modern subject is shown in its own country and decade.
- The script itself (length, number of scenes, the hook, labels, `facts_used`, chapters, the send line) stays exactly as the rules above say.

THE FIRST 30 SECONDS (founder, 23 Nov 2026: "same changes we need in long videos also ... views and watching hour is an ultimate target"; this OVERRIDES any slower opening above):
- The first four scenes are written in short beats: sentences of two to seven words, one fact each, at most 18 words of `vo` in a scene.
- Scene 1: the single strangest fact of the whole video, stated flat, with its number or name. No greeting, no background, no "aaj hum", no "imagine".
- Scene 2: the contradiction ("Lekin granth kuch aur kehta hai." / "Par bank yeh nahi batata.").
- Scene 3: what the viewer stands to lose or learn, in one line about them.
- Scene 4: an open question the video answers only near the end, and a promise of one specific thing that is coming ("Aur aakhir mein, woh ek kagaz jo sab badal deta hai."). The promise must be kept in the video.
- No explanation, list of dates or definition before second 30. A different picture for each of these four scenes.
- Everything in these scenes is a fact from the research, labelled as the rules above say; a strong opening is never an exaggeration.

TRAGEDIES AND DISASTERS (24 Nov 2026): when the subject is a disaster, an accident, a crime or a death, the telling is sober and respectful. Explain what happened and why, and what changed afterwards; name the dead only as the sources do, with numbers given as reported. No cliff-hanger built on someone's death, no "aap hairaan reh jayenge", no jokes, no shock emoji in the title or caption, no graphic picture (show the place, the documents, the memorial, people from behind). Never tie it to a different, recent event or guess at a cause the fact sheet does not give. The founder's opening line states the question plainly; it does not sensationalise.

MYTHS OF THE WORLD, IN A LONG VIDEO (SG-MYTH-WORLD; founder, 24 Nov 2026: "myth stories of around the world ... sharing myth with reason"): the subject may be any widely held story or belief from any country. Tell the myth first, as people tell it, across the opening chapters (label legend, or scripture when a named text says it); then the reason, chapter by chapter: what the text really says, what a dig found, what science measured. A finding from science or a study takes the label "archaeology" (shown on screen as "Khoj", the finding); where experts disagree, "disputed". Keep the story moving: people act and speak, each chapter ends on the next question, and no chapter is a list of editions, scholars or digs. A faith is never called false and never mocked: the belief and the evidence are laid side by side.

PACE (founder, 27 Nov 2026: "Do all 3", after the week's most-viewed short videos were watched: every narrator was brisk and dramatic). Write for a fast, tense telling. Short sentences, most of them under ten words. One new fact or turn in every sentence; never two sentences that say the same thing. End a scene on a turn that makes the next one necessary ("Par asli baat ab aati hai." is NOT such a turn: name the thing that is about to change). Use the active voice and name who did what. No warm-up, no "Aaiye dekhte hain", no "Toh chaliye". The facts, numbers and sources stay exactly as the fact sheet gives them: pace comes from how it is told, never from adding or sharpening a claim.

THE SHORT INSIDE THE LONG (founder, 28 Nov 2026: "one long script should be written in a way that at least 1 short can be made out of it"). One run of 5 to 8 consecutive scenes, anywhere after the opening, must stand alone as a complete Short: it opens on a question a stranger would ask, answers it, and holds the single most surprising fact of the video. Inside that run, name the subject in full and never lean on an earlier scene ("as we saw", "that same king"). It is still a natural part of the long; nothing is repeated for its sake. Give the run in `short_section` as [first scene number, last scene number], counting the first scene of the script as 1.
