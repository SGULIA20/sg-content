

REALISTIC PICTURES (founder, 11 Nov 2026: the scripts stay exactly as the rules above say; only the pictures change):
- EVERY scene's `visual.prompt` describes one REALISTIC, photographic-looking view of real Indian life (a bank counter, a kirana shop, a family at the dining table, a crowded street, a hand holding a passbook, a locked shutter) with natural light: say "photorealistic" and describe the place, the Indian people and the moment. No cartoon, comic or drawing words, no named cast characters, no drawn arrows, no text, numbers, screens, stock boards, charts or logos in the picture.
- Length, number of scenes, the hook, the beats, `facts_used`, the disclaimer and the send line all stay as the rules above say.

PACE (founder, 27 Nov 2026: "Do all 3", after the week's most-viewed short videos were watched: every narrator was brisk and dramatic). Write for a fast, tense telling. Short sentences, most of them under ten words. One new fact or turn in every sentence; never two sentences that say the same thing. End a scene on a turn that makes the next one necessary ("Par asli baat ab aati hai." is NOT such a turn: name the thing that is about to change). Use the active voice and name who did what. No warm-up, no "Aaiye dekhte hain", no "Toh chaliye". The facts, numbers and sources stay exactly as the fact sheet gives them: pace comes from how it is told, never from adding or sharpening a claim.

FACTS BEFORE FEELINGS (founder, 28 Nov 2026, on the 2,000 note Short: "where were the facts ... worst script we uploaded ever"). The fact sheet is the story; the script must SPEAK it. Put the single strongest figure or date of the sheet in the hook or the second line, and carry at least three different numbers or years in the spoken lines (in digits, with the plain-word meaning beside each: "97 percent wapas aaye, yaani 100 mein se 97"). At least half the scenes must state a fact from the sheet and list it in `facts_used`. Never invent an event to set a scene (a shopkeeper who refused a note, a family at a counter) unless a fact on the sheet says it happened; a human moment may only be a question or "socho ...". A claim that is the heart of the video (for example that a note stays legal tender) must itself be a fact on the sheet. Short sentences still apply; a figure is one more short sentence, not a lecture.
