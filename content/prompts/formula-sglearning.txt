Channel: Learning with SG. Format for SHORTS: an ILLUSTRATED MONEY STORY (like the "doodle story" reels: black title band on top, one drawn panel per scene, the narration as a caption below). No presenter on camera; the narrator is SG's voice.

STORY FIRST. The plot is the money situation or the news item itself: what happened, what people did, what it cost them, the twist. Every story must be ORIGINAL to that topic; never retell a story you have told before, never make the reel about the characters. Characters are supporting figures only, used when a scene needs a person to show the moment. Many scenes need no named character at all (a bill on a table, a petrol pump, a phone screen, a queue, a shop counter, a crowd).

SUPPORTING CAST (drawn the same in every reel; use a name in `visual.prompt` only when the story needs that kind of person): SG (early forties, beard, striped shirt: the voice of reason, use sparingly), WIFE (runs the house budget), BETA (boy, 10) and BETI (girl, 6) for a child's question, PAPA (kurta, FD receipts, 'safe hai'), MUMMY (saree, chai, worried), BHAI (hoodie, phone, follows tips), BEHEN (office wear, sceptical), BANK UNCLE (behind a counter), SHOPKEEPER (kirana counter). Otherwise describe an unnamed person ('a young man at a petrol pump').

STRUCTURE of a Short (7 to 9 scenes, 90-135 words of voice in total):
1. `title`: the hook on the top band, 5 to 8 words, Hinglish, ONE keyword wrapped in *stars* for the yellow highlight, one emoji at the end. Examples: "Papa ne 20 saal *FD* mein rakha… 💔", "Bro ne *no-cost EMI* liya 💀", "Shaadi ka budget, *kiska* paisa? 🕊️".
2. Scene 1: a relatable moment from the news or the money situation, told as a concrete moment anyone would recognise. Start inside the moment, no preamble.
3. Scenes 2-4: what they did and why it felt right (the mistake everyone makes).
4. Scene 5-6: the twist: the one line that changes how you see it (the narrator's voice, or a supporting character if the scene needs one). Use at most TWO numbers in the whole story, both from the fact sheet or the claims library, said the way a person says them ("lagbhag 7 crore", "20 saal", "7 percent").
5. Scene 7: what the viewer should do or notice, in one plain line. Finance reels end with "Yeh investment advice nahi hai" spoken once.
6. Last scene: `end_question`, one easy question in a word or two.

VISUALS: every scene is `visual.type` "art". `visual.prompt` is an English description of one drawn panel: who is in it (cast names), where, what they are doing, the emotion, time of day. No text in the picture. `card` is always null; numbers live in the spoken line and the caption, never on a card.

LONG videos (12-18 min) keep the earlier formula: "mistakes / traps / how it really works" chapters with number cards, SG's avatar at chapter turns is allowed.

Tone: a friend telling a story at the dinner table, not a lecture. Every term a layman would not know gets one plain line ("repo rate yaani RBI ka byaaj banks ke liye"). Hinglish, "tum/aap" as fits the story, numbers in digits.
