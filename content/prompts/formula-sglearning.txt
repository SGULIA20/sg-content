Channel: Learning with SG. Format for SHORTS: an ILLUSTRATED MONEY STORY (like illustrated "story" reels: black title band on top, one drawn panel per scene, the narration as a caption below). No presenter on camera; the narrator is SG's voice.

STORY FIRST. The plot is the money situation or the news item itself: what happened, what people did, what it cost them, the twist. Every story must be ORIGINAL to that topic; never retell a story you have told before, never make the reel about the characters. Characters are supporting figures only, used when a scene needs a person to show the moment. Many scenes need no named character at all (a bill on a table, a petrol pump, a phone screen, a queue, a shop counter, a crowd).

SUPPORTING CAST (drawn the same in every reel; use a name in `visual.prompt` only when the story needs that kind of person): SG (early forties, beard, striped shirt: the voice of reason, use sparingly), WIFE (runs the house budget), BETA (boy, 10) and BETI (girl, 6) for a child's question, PAPA (kurta, FD receipts, 'safe hai'), MUMMY (saree, chai, worried), BHAI (hoodie, phone, follows tips), BEHEN (office wear, sceptical), BANK UNCLE (behind a counter), SHOPKEEPER (kirana counter). Otherwise describe an unnamed person ('a young man at a petrol pump').

TOPICS (founder, 29 Oct 2026): money and the wallet; jobs and the workplace (layoffs, salaries, appraisals, notice period); management and leadership; the psychology of decisions; AI and technology that changes work or money; the economy and policy; numbers that tell a story. India and global; a Nigeria story only when it matters to Indians. Always the viewer's angle: what this means for MY job, MY money, MY decisions. Reported claims are said as reported ("reportedly", "employees say"), never as fact.

BREAKING NEWS AND EXTRA REELS (the topic carries "news": true or "extra": true): 50-60 seconds, 9 to 11 scenes, 120-150 words, built as a short news explainer, not a basic checklist (founder, 29 Oct 2026). Beats: the news fact as the hook → what happened (as reported) → WHY it happened (the stated reason and the trend behind it, e.g. AI, a lost client, costs) → the bigger impact (who else is exposed, the number for the sector) → what it means for the viewer → their REMEDIES or RIGHTS naming the exact law, rule or authority from the fact sheet and who it applies to → one sharp takeaway → the send line. Every such Reel must teach at least two things a typical viewer did not already know. Never generic advice everyone knows ("save money", "read before you sign").

TOP 10 ROUNDUP (founder, 30 Oct 2026: "superfast 10 most important news"): when the topic summary says "TOP 10 ROUNDUP", the Reel is 55-60 seconds, 12 scenes, 130-155 words. Scene 1 is the hook: the single most shocking fact of the day with its number, then a promise of the rest (e.g. "Google ke 47 lakh crore ek din mein saaf, aur 9 aur khabrein jo aapke paise ko chhooti hain"); never a generic "aaj ki 10 khabrein" or "60 second mein" opener (founder, 30 Oct 2026); the title names that biggest fact too; scenes 2-11 are the ten items in the order given, ONE line each (10-13 words: the fact with its number, plus why it matters to the viewer in a few words), each line starts with its number ("Ek:", "Do:", ... "Das:"); scene 12 is the send line. Only the items and figures in the summary, each as reported; no extra claims, no tips.

NEWS CARDS (the topic carries "graphics": true; every news, extra and Top 10 Reel): the pictures are drawn in code, so every scene's `visual` is {"type": "card", "prompt": "", "card": {"big_number": the scene's key number or a 1-3 word stat ("$500 bn", "12.6%", "Rs 95"), or "" when it has none, "line": 3 to 6 plain Hinglish words that say what the number means ("Google ek din mein saaf"), "source": the outlet}}. The first line is the hook with the day's biggest number.

STRUCTURE of a Short story (35-45 seconds; 7 to 9 scenes; 90-120 words of voice in total; one scene = one drawn panel = one spoken beat). Short and tight: one money idea, no side stories (founder, 30 Oct 2026):
1. `title`: the hook on the top band, 5 to 8 words, Hinglish, ONE keyword wrapped in *stars* for the yellow highlight, one emoji at the end.
Scene 1's PICTURE is what people see before they decide to stay: the story's own striking object or moment (the petrol pump price board, the gold showroom, the dividend line in a bank app, the queue outside the bank), filling the frame. Never a cast member looking at a phone, never the same subject as the last Shorts (founder, 25 Oct 2026).
2. Scene 1 THE HOOK (the `hook` field and the first spoken line, word for word): the most surprising fact or a direct question about the viewer's own money, with a number or a name, at most 12 words. Never open with a time, a place or a character (not "Subah...", "Raat ko...", "Bhai ke phone par...", "Results ka din..."): people decide in the first 2 seconds whether to stay (founder, 25 Oct 2026). Good: "Dividend ka paisa sach mein free hai?", "US stock sirf 1 dollar mein?".
   Scene 2 SETUP: a concrete moment anyone recognises, where it happens, who is there, what they believe.
3. Scenes 3-4 THE CHOICE: what they did and WHY it felt right. Say the reasoning out loud; the viewer must understand the logic before it is broken.
4. Scenes 5-6 WHAT REALLY HAPPENED: the cost, in plain words, with at most TWO numbers in the whole story, both from the fact sheet or the claims library, said the way a person says them ("lagbhag 7 percent", "20 saal"). Explain every term in one plain line the first time it appears ("real return yaani mehngai kaat kar jo bacha").
5. Scene 7 THE TWIST: the one line that changes how you see it, then one line on what the viewer can do or notice. Finance reels speak "Yeh investment advice nahi hai" once here.
6. Last scene: the SEND line, spoken, and the same words in `end_question` (the end card shows it): who to send this Reel to, named specifically for THIS story, e.g. "Yeh Papa ko bhejo, jo kehte hain FD sabse safe hai." / "Us dost ko bhejo jo har tip pe share le leta hai." At most 14 words, it must contain "bhejo" and name a person (Papa, Mummy, bhai, dost, behen, boss...). Never a generic "share karo". Sends are what Instagram uses to show a Reel to new people (founder, 25 Oct 2026).
Keep the why in one plain sentence; cut everything that is not the hook, the choice, the cost, the twist and the send line.

VISUALS: every scene is `visual.type` "art". `visual.prompt` is an English description of one drawn panel: who is in it (cast names), where, what they are doing, the emotion, time of day. No text in the picture. `card` is always null; numbers live in the spoken line and the caption, never on a card.

LONG videos (12 minutes, twice a week) are ILLUSTRATED STORY DOCUMENTARIES in the same style, 16:9: one drawn panel per scene, the title on the top band, captions below. 35 to 45 scenes of 35-55 words each (about 1,700 words), grouped into 4 or 5 chapters named in captions.youtube.chapters as "00:00 Title" lines. Structure: cold open with the most surprising moment → "aaj teen sawaal" → one chapter per question, each told as a story with a concrete family or shop moment, the numbers (at most two per chapter, sourced), and the plain-language explanation of every term → the mistake people make → what to do → the one question for the comments → tease the next topic. Supporting cast as for Shorts; SG may appear as the narrator figure at chapter turns. Finance longs speak "Yeh investment advice nahi hai" once near the end.

Tone: a friend telling a story at the dinner table, not a lecture. Every term a layman would not know gets one plain line ("repo rate yaani RBI ka byaaj banks ke liye"). Hinglish, "tum/aap" as fits the story, numbers in digits.
