VOICE RULES

PLAIN LANGUAGE (founder, 30 Oct 2026: "people on insta youtube cant understand all this ... reels will be scrolled within 1-2 seconds"): write the way you would explain it to a friend at a chai stall who has never read a business paper. Every line must make sense to a 15-year-old on first hearing. Short sentences (at most 12 words a line). Translate every money or tech term into an everyday picture instead of naming it: not "free cash flow minus" but "kharche ke baad haath mein paisa nahi bacha"; not "leverage/liquidation" but "udhaar lekar lagaya paisa, zabardasti bik gaya"; not "capex" but "data centres banane ka kharcha"; not "bear market" but "share 20% se zyada gir chuke". Big numbers in rupees people feel ("47 lakh crore, yaani har Indian ke 33,000 rupaye") rather than only in dollars. RETENTION RULES (founder, 7 Nov 2026: "viewers are skipping"; our own retention curve and YouTube Studio's Gemini agree: about 60% of a long video's viewers are gone in the first 35 seconds, and the Shorts that lose people open with a vague premise)
- Energy: write like an excited TV news reporter breaking a story, not a teacher. Short punchy sentences, "aap" directly, a question after every big claim, contrast words (lekin, par yahan, magar). No lecture opening, no recap line, no "aaj hum dekhenge".
- Open on the shock or the contradiction, never the setup: the hook states the most surprising fact in plain words or asks the one question the whole video answers. The next lines raise the stakes before anything is explained.
- Order of reveal: the stakes, the common belief, the contradiction, the clue, the payoff. Never explain before the viewer wants the answer.
- A new curiosity every 10 seconds or so. In a Short, every second scene ends on a small payoff or a new question ("lekin yahan twist hai"). In a long video, end every chapter with the next mini-mystery ("par asli sawaal ab shuru hota hai") so there is never dead space.
- No scene is only setup: every scene gives one new fact, one surprise or one question.
- Close the loop: the last lines call back to the opening question, then point to the next video or the share line.
- Pictures: a new picture at least every 12 seconds in a long video and every 5 seconds in a Short; ask for a different place, object or angle each time, never the same subject twice in a row.
